\title{Zero-Query Depth-One QAOA Surfaces from Local Edge Statistics}
\author{Molena Huynh}\email{molena.huynh@jmp.com}
\affiliation{North Carolina State University}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Depth-one MaxCut QAOA admits an exact classical surface formula determined by
edge degrees and common-neighbor counts. This paper implements the formula as
a streamed zero-query baseline and evaluates it before learned singular-value
priors. Edge statistics require
$O(n+m+\sum_{(u,v)}\min\{\deg u,\deg v\})$ preprocessing; a $q$-point surface
then costs $O(mq)$ arithmetic and $O(1)$ extra storage when streamed. The
implementation is checked against independent statevectors at all 3,888 values
from 48 authenticated ten-vertex neighborhoods of Stanford Gset G1. Maximum
absolute discrepancy is $1.33\times10^{-15}$. Group-isomorphism analysis shows
that these 48 tasks form only 16 classes and that 38 of 48 assignments in a
superseded row split have an isomorphic training task. Class-grouped folds
remove that leakage. At one queried target value, mean normalized regret is
0.01572 for a training mean, 0.00920 for ridge, and 0.00791 for a singular-value
prior; the exact formula has zero regret at every budget while using no target
objective queries. The result replaces a query-efficiency claim by an exact
computational baseline and a collision-aware evaluation contract. It applies
to unweighted depth one, not higher depth or hardware execution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Depth-one objective and source tasks}

For an unweighted graph $G=(V,E)$, define
\begin{equation}
 C=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\frac{I-Z_uZ_v}{2},\qquad B=\sum_{v\in V}X_v,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 F_G(\gamma,\beta)=|E|^{-1}\langle+|e^{i\gamma C}e^{i\beta B}
 Ce^{-i\beta B}e^{-i\gamma C}|+\rangle. \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The registered grid contains nine values of each angle. Forty-eight tasks are
deterministic ten-vertex breadth-first neighborhoods of the checksum-pinned
Stanford Gset G1 graph~\cite{gset}. They are related samples from one graph,
not independent graph draws.

\begin{theorem}[Isomorphism invariance]
If $G'$ is obtained by a vertex permutation, then
$F_{G'}(\gamma,\beta)=F_G(\gamma,\beta)$ for every angle pair and every QAOA
depth. Isomorphic tasks cannot be separated across an independence-based
train--test split.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $U_\pi$ permute qubits. Then $C_{G'}=U_\pi C_GU_\pi^\dagger$,
$U_\pi BU_\pi^\dagger=B$, and $U_\pi|+\rangle^{\otimes n}=|+\rangle^{\otimes n}$.
Conjugating every layer and the measured cost in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} by
$U_\pi$ leaves the scalar expectation unchanged.
\end{proof}

Exact isomorphism testing gives class sizes
$11,7,7,5,4,2,2,2,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1$. Four grouped folds contain 12 tasks each.
The old row folds place an isomorphic training task beside 38 of 48 test
assignments; Fig.~\ref{fig:collision} reports the full overlap audit.

\section{Exact streamed surface}

For edge $(u,v)$, set $d_u=\deg(u)-1$, $d_v=\deg(v)-1$, and
$\lambda_{uv}=|N(u)\cap N(v)|$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact depth-one edge value]
\label{thm:formula}
At depth one,
\begin{align}
\langle C_{uv}\rangle={}&\frac12+\frac14\sin(4\beta)\sin\gamma
(\cos^{d_u}\gamma+\cos^{d_v}\gamma)\nonumber\\
&-\frac14\sin^2(2\beta)\cos^{d_u+d_v-2\lambda_{uv}}\!\gamma
[1-\cos^{\lambda_{uv}}(2\gamma)]. \label{eq:formula}
\end{align}
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:formula} and dividing by $m=|E|$ gives the exact
objective. With hashed adjacency, preprocessing costs
$O(n+m+\sum_{(u,v)}\min\{\deg u,\deg v\})$ and $O(n+m)$ storage. A $q$-point
surface costs $O(mq)$ work and may be streamed in $O(1)$ extra storage.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Mixer conjugation gives
\begin{equation}
e^{i\beta B}Z_uZ_ve^{-i\beta B}=c^2Z_uZ_v+cs(Z_uY_v+Y_uZ_v)+s^2Y_uY_v,
\end{equation}
with $c=\cos(2\beta)$ and $s=\sin(2\beta)$. Cost terms outside the closed
neighborhood of $(u,v)$ commute and cancel. Conjugating the remaining Pauli
strings and using $\langle+|P|+\rangle=0$ unless
$P\in\{I,X\}^{\otimes n}$ leaves the two endpoint contributions and one
common-neighbor contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:formula}; this is the established
Wang--Hadfield--Jiang--Rieffel identity~\cite{wang2018fermionic}. Degrees follow
from one adjacency pass, while intersecting the smaller endpoint-neighbor set
costs $O(\min\{\deg u,\deg v\})$ per edge. Formula evaluation is constant work
per edge and angle pair.
\end{proof}

statevector evaluation and reports the matched runtime scaling.

\section{Grouped query audit}

Each test surface is ranked by a predicted surface. Regret after querying the
first $b$ ranked target values is
\begin{equation}
r_b=\frac{\max_\theta F(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in Q_b}F(\theta)}
{\max_\theta F(\theta)}.
\end{equation}
This finite-grid statistic does not assert continuous global optimality.

\section{Scope}

The exact baseline is specific to unweighted depth-one MaxCut and a fixed
angle grid. It does not extend unchanged to higher depth, weighted objectives,
noise, or hardware sampling. The authenticated tasks come from one Gset graph.
Within that scope, every learned query-advantage claim is dominated by the
zero-query formula, and row-wise evaluation is invalidated by graph collisions.
The useful output is therefore the streamed formula implementation, its
resource bound, and an isomorphism-grouped testing protocol.
